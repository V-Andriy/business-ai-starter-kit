# Research Brief

## First Output

Produce a short answer or comparison table with source links, a recommendation when requested, and the most important unknown. Use the current request; do not create a project folder for a one-off question.

## Scope

Identify the decision, options, criteria, geography, and time horizon. Ask only for missing criteria that could reverse the answer. State reasonable assumptions and proceed with a first pass.

## Evidence

Use supplied sources first. Verify changing claims with current primary sources when available. Record dates for prices and policy claims. Keep observations, estimates, and inferences separate. If access fails, state the gap instead of inventing evidence.

## Synthesis

Compare options against the same criteria. Explain the main tradeoff and what new evidence could change the recommendation. A brief normally needs the answer, evidence, options, and next decision; avoid an oversized research dump.

## Quality Check

Check that every consequential factual claim has support, links match the claim, figures use comparable periods and units, and uncertainty is visible. Do not present marketing claims as independently verified facts. Financial, legal, or medical decisions need domain-appropriate verification.

## Boundary

Do not buy reports, connect accounts, transfer private data, or contact sources without authorization. Keep research notes in the existing project only when the work will continue.
